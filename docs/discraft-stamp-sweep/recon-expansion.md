No retailer blocked or rate-limited this survey. Public page and image reads were deliberately paced (typically 5–8 seconds between repeated requests, with longer inspection intervals); catalog and image passes overlapped across different sources. No challenge was solved, certificate warning bypassed, URL resolution variants guessed, or blocked source retried. The Python reader could not validate Disc Golf Center’s certificate chain; the ordinary browser loaded it without a warning, and its four-page catalog and Nuke original were inspected there.

This is a catalog/source reconnaissance, **not an exhaustive audit of every individual SKU photo, sold-disc archive or historical run**. Out-of-stock catalog listings count. Search results can contain other plastics, related molds or generic product education; none establishes ESP by itself. The early Reaper/G3T searches with negative Swirl/FLX/Glo terms were superseded by the unexcluded passes above and supply no negative-availability conclusions. G3T’s final pass explicitly excluded used entries; Foundation’s separate generic used Nuke was also checked but did not establish a named plain-ESP variant.

Plain **ESP** remains required. Explicit Signature ESP, ESP Swirl/Swirly ESP, Tour Series ESP, Special Blend ESP, Glo, FLX, recycled, misprint and other ESP-family variants are excluded, even when the retailer abbreviates the product title to “ESP.” A player signature on ordinary ESP stock artwork does not itself change the plastic: the selected page/run must identify ESP, as with Nuke, Force, Buzzz and Cigarra. Cosmetic swirls in plain ESP are not a substitute for checking the listed plastic.
